## Strongest Miller North candidate

`A.H. v. Fraser Health Authority`, 2019 BCSC 227, is direct healthcare legal evidence involving an Indigenous woman and prolonged hospital detention. The judgment addresses statutory and Charter safeguards. It does not make a finding of Indigenous-specific racism. Because the record is sensitive and not yet reconciled to a public incident, it remains private owner review.

## Housing and practical-support context

`Smith v. Mohan (No. 2)`, 2020 BCHRT 52, is a merits finding involving discrimination against an Indigenous tenant. `Abel v. Faraja Mwenebembe`, 2021 AHRC 5, is only a procedural decision allowing a complaint to proceed. These roles must remain distinct. Both may inform general Indigenous legal-navigation and tenancy-support matching; neither is a healthcare incident.

For Miller, `Victoria (City) v. Adams`, 2009 BCCA 563, and `Abbotsford (City) v. Shantz`, 2015 BCSC 1909, provide constitutional homelessness context. `Alberta (Human Rights and Citizenship Commission) v. Kellogg Brown & Root (Canada) Company`, 2007 ABCA 426, is a limitation case distinguishing recreational drug use from a proved or perceived addiction disability on that record. The cases are context, not individualized legal advice.

## Mental-health legal context

`British Columbia (Attorney General) v. Council of Canadians with Disabilities`, 2022 SCC 27, granted public-interest standing for a challenge to B.C. mental-health legislation. It did not decide the constitutional merits of non-consensual treatment provisions.
