# Miller Addictions Cross-Chain Synthesis

Date: 2026-09-05
Scope: private benchmark and owner review only
Inputs: 21 chains, 73 policy/legal records, 30 commitments, 68 source-catalog entries, 15 policy-to-service candidates

## Result

The 21 chains produce 12 defensible cross-chain pattern candidates and six overlapping topic clusters. The strongest repeated structure is:

> systemic finding or recommendation → government response or funding → formal rule/program → regional service evidence → later audit, utilization report, or renewed recommendation

That structure is not universal. Legal and professional-standard chains usually expose clear issue/effective/amendment dates. Funding and bed-capacity chains more often stop at an aggregate total that cannot be reconciled to a stable facility inventory.

The 30 commitments remain distributed as eight implemented, 12 partially implemented, six underway, three with no clear evidence found, and one unclear. Those labels describe documented implementation scope; they are not findings about effectiveness.

## Recurring themes

1. **Access is the dominant cross-chain theme.** OAT, adult and youth treatment, First Nations-led care, withdrawal management, and system-of-care chains all contain access or capacity commitments.
2. **Standards and evaluation recur.** Coroner, audit, professional-regulator, and federal expert materials repeatedly call for standards, monitoring, evaluation, or accountable system design.
3. **Operational evidence is decentralized.** Ministry sources establish policy and funding; health-authority and operator directories are usually better evidence that a named service is actually available.
4. **Aggregate reporting creates a reconciliation gap.** Provincial bed/site totals cannot always be traced to individual operators, locations, eligibility rules, and current access routes.
5. **Rural, remote, and northern access recurs.** The documented responses include virtual OAT, transportation, satellite clinics, Nation-led beds, and planned northern facilities. These sources do not establish geographic adequacy.
6. **First Nations governance must remain explicit.** Provincial funding, FNHA support, and local First Nations governance are distinct roles; a funded Nation-led service is not thereby a provincial program.
7. **Formal standards are easier to version.** Pharmacy, nursing, prescribing, and assisted-living standards expose dates and supersession more consistently than program announcements.

## High-value review signals

- Five chains form a prolonged-partial-implementation cluster: system of care, prescribed alternatives, coroner recommendations, treatment capacity, and the harm-reduction audit.
- Seven repeated/revisited recommendation signals were retained. Four are strong substantive repetitions; three require more interpretive caution because the responsible body, scope, or proposed mechanism changed.
- Twelve of the 15 service links can be operationally verified, but only seven resolve to an existing curated Miller resource by identity evidence.
- The local canonical `resource_registry` contains no rows, so no production UUID match is asserted.

## Topic clusters

The deterministic clusters are treatment/service access; toxic-drug and overdose response; OAT/professional practice; prescribed alternatives/harm reduction; law/regulation/formal rules; and Indigenous-led/rural access. Chains may belong to multiple clusters.

## Safe interpretation

Repeated recommendations are review signals, not proof that an institution failed. Funding is not operation. Operation is not utilization. Utilization is not effectiveness. Later incidents or mortality evidence do not establish that a reform caused, prevented, or failed to prevent an outcome.
