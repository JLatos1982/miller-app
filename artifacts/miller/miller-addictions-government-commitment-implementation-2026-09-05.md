Nine commitment records require owner review. Seven are repeated-recommendation signals.

## Strong confirmed implementation examples

- Ministerial Order 488 required overdose-prevention services; more than 20 sites were documented operating within weeks and later provincial counts show sustained scale.
- Provincial drug checking launched a distributed model in 2021; government later reported 128 access sites.
- The provincewide OAT Access Line launched with a published number, operating hours and clinical pathway.
- Nurse OAT prescribing progressed from emergency scope expansion and training cohorts to an in-force regulator standard.
- OPS minimum service standards were published and the Province states they were implemented provincewide in 2025.
- The 105-bed treatment/recovery commitment was followed by an official statement that all 105 were operational.
- Twelve First Nations-led beds were reported open in Adams Lake, Skidegate and Old Massett.
- A revised prescribed-alternatives policy was published in 2025 after the 2024 PHO review.

## Strong partial examples

- OAT access expanded through professional standards, workforce, coverage and virtual access, but the benchmark did not establish needs-based provincial or iOAT adequacy.
- Prescribed alternatives reached operational delivery, but the Auditor General documented barriers and insufficient public performance reporting.
- Road to Recovery operates in several regions, but the announced 100-bed expansion and all regional access lines have not been fully reconciled.
- The 2023 Budget bed commitments overlap later program announcements; individual facilities must be reconciled before treating aggregate totals as completion.
- B.C.'s decriminalization pilot partly reflected PHO and federal task-force recommendations, then was narrowed; it was never national implementation.

## No clear evidence found

- A publication exactly matching the Coroners Service request for a monitored 30/60/90-day illicit-drug-toxicity action plan.
- Implementation of the 2023 recommendation for alternatives that do not require an individual prescription.
- A comprehensive national service-standard instrument matching the federal expert task force recommendation.

“No clear evidence found” is a search result, not a finding of noncompliance.

## Implementation evidence hierarchy

Operational service pages, regulator effective-date notices, implementation reports, audits and evaluations were treated as stronger evidence than announcements. Activity totals were kept separate from adequacy, utilization from outcome, and outcome from causal effectiveness.
