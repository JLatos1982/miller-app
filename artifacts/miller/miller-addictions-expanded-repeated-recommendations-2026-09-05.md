# Expanded Repeated-Recommendation Review

Date: 2026-09-05
Scope: private owner-review artifact

## Summary

Seven recommendation signals were examined longitudinally. Four are high-confidence repetitions or direct revisitations; three are related later recommendations/strategies whose scope changed enough to require caution. Repetition is treated as an unresolved-research signal, not proof of non-implementation.

| Topic | Original → later | Evidence between | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regulation and oversight of treatment/recovery | 2018 coroner panel → 2022 panel | 2019 Assisted Living Regulation; 2021 supportive-recovery standards | Substantially similar recommendation repeated after partial rule implementation. Owner review is required because rules do not prove complete oversight or outcomes monitoring. |
| OAT/iOAT access | 2018 panel → 2022 panel | Documented service and prescribing expansion | The 2022 panel said the earlier access recommendation remained relevant and required; implementation existed but the access concern continued. |
| Drug checking | 2018 panel → 2022 panel | Distributed drug-checking implementation reported in 2021 | Recommendation repeated despite rollout; this may concern scale or access, not absence of service. |
| Safer supply | 2022 panel → 2023 urgent panel | Prescribed program and enrolment evidence | Later panel revisited the toxic-supply concern but proposed non-prescribed alternatives, a materially different path. |
| Evidence-based continuum of care | 2022 panel → 2023 urgent panel | Adult System of Care Framework published in 2022 | Later report documented continued crisis and referred back to 2022, but it did not literally repeat the entire continuum recommendation. |
| Decriminalization | 2019 PHO → 2021 federal expert task force | No exemption yet in force | Substantially similar direction from a different authoritative body; a B.C. exemption followed in 2023. |
| National service standards | 2021 federal expert task force → 2023 federal drug strategy | Federal response and strategy work | The later strategy revisited coordination, but no single national service-standard instrument was located. |

## Strongest direct evidence

The [2022 BC Coroners Service report](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/birth-adoption-death-marriage-and-divorce/deaths/coroners-service/death-review-panel/review_of_illicit_drug_toxicity_deaths_2022.pdf) states that the recommendations from the earlier panel remained relevant and required. The [2023 urgent-response report](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/birth-adoption-death-marriage-and-divorce/deaths/coroners-service/death-review-panel/an_urgent_response_to_a_continuing_crisis_report.pdf) explicitly focuses on the 2022 safer-supply recommendation.

## Owner-review decisions

- Keep regulation/oversight, drug-checking scale, safer-supply mechanism, continuum-of-care recurrence, and national service standards review-gated.
- Do not collapse prescribed and non-prescribed alternatives into one recommendation.
- Do not treat a repeated recommendation as proof that no implementation occurred; several chains show implementation activity between reports.
- Preserve the source organization and recipient separately because repetition by a new body may indicate convergence rather than a failed response.
