# Coroner and Audit Longitudinal Recommendation Review

Date: 2026-09-05
Scope: systemic/aggregate public material only

## Sources inspected

- 2018 BC Coroners Service illicit-drug overdose death-review panel
- 2022 BC Coroners Service illicit-drug toxicity death-review panel
- 2023 BC Coroners Service urgent-response panel
- 2022 recommendation-response portal and ministry/regulator responses
- 2024 Office of the Auditor General report on overdose-prevention/supervised-consumption services and prescribed safer supply

No unnecessary information about individual deceased people was collected.

## Longitudinal history

### Treatment regulation and oversight

The 2018 panel called for provincial regulation, evidence-based quality, monitoring, and evaluation of treatment and recovery programs. The Assisted Living Regulation and later supportive-recovery standards are concrete rule changes. The 2022 panel nevertheless stated the earlier recommendations remained relevant and required. Status: partial implementation documented; completion of the broader oversight/evaluation objective remains unclear.

### OAT and continuum of care

The 2018 panel called for expanded OAT/iOAT and recovery supports. The 2022 panel again called for an evidence-based continuum of care. Subsequent evidence includes prescribing-scope changes, professional standards, an OAT access line, a provincial framework, and regional service expansion. Status: implementation activity is clear; the recurring access recommendation remains a review signal.

### Drug checking and overdose prevention

The 2018 panel called for provincial drug-checking access. Later progress reporting documents distributed services, and overdose-prevention services have formal minimum standards and utilization reporting. The later panel's statement that earlier recommendations remained relevant may concern reach or scale; it does not show that no implementation occurred.

### Safer supply / prescribed alternatives

The 2022 panel recommended access to a safer supply. Program and enrolment evidence followed. The 2023 panel revisited the issue through a materially different non-prescribed model. The 2024 Auditor General report documented implementation gaps in the prescribed program. Status: partial implementation with a repeated but changed recommendation.

## Audit role

The Auditor General provides a distinct evidence function: it compares program implementation against objectives rather than merely documenting announcements. Its March 2024 report is therefore a stronger implementation-depth source than a funding release, while still not establishing causal effectiveness.

## Research implications

- Preserve each panel, response, rule change, service implementation, and audit as a separate stage.
- Attach recommendation recipients explicitly.
- Mark repeated recommendations as review signals and link the implementation evidence that occurred between reports.
- Search forward from each official response for dated deliverables, not just acceptance language.
