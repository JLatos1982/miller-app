# Research Brief — Connecting public policy and funding to addiction services

## Executive summary

This private pilot tested whether Miller can trace selected addiction services back through government initiatives, commitments and funding, then forward to current operation or use. Six resources produced complete private backstories and 18 source-backed edges. The evidence is strongest when a dated announcement, a named operator record and a current service or utilization source converge.

## Scope

Six British Columbia resources were selected from previously verified policy-to-service candidates: Creekside, Access Central, Oak Care, Maple Ridge Treatment Centre, Inlet Recovery Home and the Opioid Treatment Access Line.

## Key findings

- Creekside, Maple Ridge and Access Central connect existing curated records to named implementation evidence.
- The Opioid Treatment Access Line is supported by launch, current access and utilization sources.
- Oak Care is operationally supported but needs address and future-relocation reconciliation.
- Inlet is listed and institutionally identified, but its exact first-intake date was not established.
- No wider program allocation was presented as resource-specific funding.

## Evidence highlights

- Fraser Health identifies ten added Creekside beds and four added Maple Ridge youth beds.
- B.C.'s 2024/25 annual report gives Road to Recovery use data after launch.
- VCH identifies the Oak Care relocation and maintains a current access page.

## Policy/service relationships

- Road to Recovery supports service creation, expansion and regional access pathways.
- The 123-youth-bed commitment is linked to four named Maple Ridge beds.
- Current operation and utilization remain separate implementation depths.

## Implementation status

Five resources have current operational evidence; Inlet has a current official listing but remains qualified because the announced first-patient milestone was not precisely confirmed. This is not an effectiveness evaluation.

## Unresolved questions

- What is Oak Care's canonical public address and successor timeline?
- When did Inlet accept its first patient?
- How should Miller represent provincewide virtual access services?
- Can any authoritative source allocate funding to a specific selected service?

## Method

The pilot began with existing private policy-to-resource candidates, preferred registered official sources, assigned a source role to each relationship edge, and retained uncertainty and publication gates. Production resources were inspected read-only.

## Sources

- Fraser Health facility and implementation pages
- Vancouver Coastal Health service and relocation pages
- Island Health service directory
- B.C. government announcements, Ministry of Health pages and annual reports

## Limitations

The sample is purposive and small. An authoritative listing demonstrates public availability information but does not by itself establish an exact opening date, complete implementation or policy effectiveness.
