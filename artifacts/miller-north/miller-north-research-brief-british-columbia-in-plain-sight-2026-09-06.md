# In Plain Sight: Public Evidence of Implementation Across 24 Recommendations

> Private research brief for owner review. Not published.

## Executive summary

The 2020 In Plain Sight review documented widespread Indigenous-specific racism and discrimination in B.C. health care and issued 24 recommendations. Public records now show many concrete institutional responses, but implementation evidence remains distributed and is not the same as evidence of effectiveness. In Plain Sight produced demonstrable institutional change, but public evidence of activity is much stronger than public evidence of system-wide outcomes.

## Scope

This brief follows one documented Miller North subject through accountability, government, policy or law, implementation and later public evidence. It includes only source-backed dimensions and does not provide legal advice.

## Timeline

- 2020-11-30: In Plain Sight published — The independent review reported systemic Indigenous-specific racism and issued 24 recommendations.
- 2021-11-30: First-anniversary response — Government reported early structures and correctly associated planned health-sector PIDA coverage with recommendation 11.
- 2022-11-29: Cultural Safety and Humility Standard released — FNHA and HSO released a First Nations-led B.C. standard.
- 2023-06: PIDA coverage extended — Official reporting states that health-authority employees came within statutory disclosure protections.
- 2023-11-30: 24-month implementation report — The task team published the most complete official recommendation-level implementation baseline located.
- 2024: Tripartite governance evaluation — An Indigenous-led evaluation documented governance progress and remaining sustainability, clarity and accountability work.
- 2026-09-06 accessed: Distributed current reporting — Action 3.07 reports activity but says capacity to report comprehensively across all 24 recommendations is limited.

## Key findings

- The current Action 3.07 page appears to mislabel the PIDA extension as recommendation 2; the original report and multiple official updates map it to recommendation 11.
- Eighteen of 24 recommendation rows still require owner review because public evidence is partial, distributed or wider in scope than the demonstrated action.
- The strongest outputs are statutory coverage, a senior ministry role and durable governance/standard-setting mechanisms; system outcomes remain less completely reported.
- Treat the tracker as public-evidence coverage, not a legal-compliance or effectiveness rating.
- Seek a ministry-owned recommendation-by-recommendation status export before any public use.

## Government, policy and accountability

- The review documents the systemic concern and findings.
- The review issues 24 recommendations.
- Official sources map the health-sector PIDA extension to recommendation 11.
- The standard is created; full institutional adoption and accreditation use are not demonstrated.
- Regulatory modernization is reported as part of the response context.
- The feedback principles operationalize part of the complaint-access work.
- The oversight program carries forward access and engagement functions.
- Current reporting does not provide one comprehensive recommendation-by-recommendation status ledger.

## Implementation

- policy_adoption: Health-sector PIDA coverage and professional-regulation changes are officially documented.
- office_or_program_creation: Senior ministry and oversight functions were created or expanded.
- operational_implementation: Patient-feedback, facility and institutional mechanisms provide concrete but non-universal operational examples.
- evaluation: The Indigenous-led tripartite evaluation documents both progress and continuing governance work.
- reporting: Current government reporting explicitly acknowledges limitations in comprehensive 24-recommendation reporting.

## Unresolved questions

- Whether a newer comprehensive official 24-row ledger exists outside public search.
- How consistently institution-level changes are operationalized.
- Which activities have measured outcomes rather than activity counts.
- How responsibility is allocated where recommendations name many institutions.

## Limitations

This case measures the public evidence located for implementation activity. It does not measure legal compliance, institutional effectiveness, patient outcomes or the lived experience of every Indigenous person in B.C. health care.
