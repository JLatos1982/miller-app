## Strengths

- Government, regulator, academic, health-system, and Indigenous-led families are all represented.
- Indigenous-led sources provide primary governance and accountability evidence, including First Nations government statements, Indigenous health governance, advocacy, and Indigenous journalism.
- Research & Policy gives primary official implementation material more prominence than the legacy incident grid alone did.
- Live Listening includes Indigenous journalism and First Nations government sources while anonymizing ordinary individuals.

## Gaps

- The large “other public or organizational” family needs future source-registry refinement; it likely contains additional health-system, advocacy, and institutional subfamilies.
- Direct tribunal, court, and human-rights source coverage is thin in the Evidence Library projection even where procedural reporting exists.
- Alberta has less Indigenous-led implementation evaluation in the current three-case material than B.C. or Saskatchewan.
- Saskatchewan has good Indigenous journalism and program evidence but no current public ten-action status ledger for the Saskatoon review.
- Legacy public summaries sometimes foreground government or institutional language when an Indigenous-led source could add governance or lived-context evidence.

## Indigenous-led sources are not merely corroboration

When an Indigenous government, First Nations health organization, or Indigenous-led oversight body is exercising governance, issuing a standard, documenting its own program, or making a formal accountability statement, Miller North should classify that source by its primary role. It should not be demoted to commentary simply because a government source also exists.

## Recommended source work

Prioritize a bounded source-registry cleanup for the 105 “other” URLs and an Indigenous-led source review of the 15 Live Listening items. Numeric equality is not the goal; the goal is appropriate authority, perspective, and role classification.
