# Saskatoon coerced sterilization / tubal-ligation cohort

> Private owner-review dossier. No publication or production mutation is authorized.

## What happened

A Saskatoon Health Region-commissioned external review documented the reported experiences of seven Indigenous women who described pressure or coercion around tubal ligation. The review treated those accounts as a cohort/systemic concern, issued ten Calls to Action, and was followed by an apology, legal proceedings and a hospital-based Indigenous birth-support response.

## Why this matters

- The case shows how anonymized lived experience can become a formal health-system accountability record without identifying patients.
- It connects institutional findings to a concrete operational service while preserving that one program does not satisfy all ten Calls to Action.
- Later litigation and federal legislation must be kept procedurally and temporally distinct from the 2017 review.

## The one thing to remember

A real, current birth-support service is documented, but it should not be mistaken for completion of the review's wider structural agenda.

## Review first

- The 2017 review's ten Calls to Action remain the canonical accountability baseline.
- The Indigenous Birth Support Worker program is a verified operational response, but it is only a partial response to a broader set of recommended structural reforms.
- Popp v Canada certification was dismissed in 2025 on class-definition/common-issue/preferability grounds; that is not a merits finding that the reported sterilizations did or did not occur.

## Review next

- No current public ten-action implementation ledger was located.
- Confirm whether an appeal followed the 2025 certification decision.

## Timeline

- 2015-2016 — **Public concern emerges.** Reported patient experiences prompted scrutiny; the dossier preserves those experiences as anonymized cohort evidence.
- 2017-01 to 2017-07 — **External review conducted.** The commissioned review interviewed seven women and nine health providers.
- 2017-07 — **Report issues ten Calls to Action.** The report documented themes of profiling, powerlessness and coercion and recommended structural reforms.
- 2017-07 — **Health-region apology reported.** The health region publicly apologized after release of the review.
- 2017-2024 — **Proposed class proceeding develops.** Litigation proceeded through pleadings and interlocutory questions, including a 2024 intervention decision.
- 2019-06 — **House health committee reports.** The federal committee reviewed coerced sterilization evidence and made federal recommendations.
- 2019-12 — **Indigenous Birth Support Worker Program begins.** SHA identifies the program as a response to the 2017 report.
- 2019-12 to 2021-01 — **Program evaluation documents use.** The evaluation reports operational learning and recommendations for continuation and scale.
- 2025-06-24 — **Class certification dismissed.** The court dismissed certification; the procedural decision did not decide the truth of each alleged sterilization claim.
- 2026-06-15 — **Bill S-228 receives royal assent.** Federal Criminal Code clarification is later contextual legal development and is not treated as a retroactive case remedy.

## What changed

- acknowledgement: The institutional apology is documented through official/parliamentary context but is not implementation of all Calls to Action.
- office_or_program_creation: SHA states that the Indigenous Birth Support Worker Program was created in response to the review.
- operational_implementation: A current SHA page confirms the service remains accessible at Jim Pattison Children's Hospital.
- evaluation: SHA reported evaluation findings and improvement/scale recommendations.

## What we know

- The commissioned review records seven anonymized patient accounts and interviews with nine providers.
- The report issued ten Calls to Action spanning rights, training, governance, workforce, supports, reparation, reproductive support and consent policy.
- SHA states that the birth-support program was created in response and is operational at Jim Pattison Children's Hospital.
- A 2025 court decision dismissed class certification at that procedural stage.

## What remains uncertain

- Current implementation status for most of the ten Calls to Action.
- Whether an appeal or successor collective proceeding followed the 2025 decision.
- Whether consent-policy changes were adopted and audited across Saskatchewan.
- How the legal claims will proceed, if at all, as individual actions.
