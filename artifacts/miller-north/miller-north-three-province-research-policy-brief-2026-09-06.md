# Miller North Research & Policy: Three Provincial Accountability Cases

> Private research brief. Prepared for review; not a publication decision.

## Executive summary

Miller North now presents three source-grounded cases showing how documented Indigenous healthcare concerns can move into reviews, recommendations, government or health-system responses, formal policy and legal context, operational implementation and later reporting. The cases are not compared by severity. They show three different public-accountability structures and a shared limitation: public sources generally document selected actions more clearly than they document recommendation-by-recommendation outcomes.

## Scope

The experience covers:

- British Columbia — *In Plain Sight* and public evidence concerning its 24 recommendations.
- Saskatchewan — the Saskatoon coerced sterilization/tubal-ligation cohort, external review, ten Calls to Action, a service response and later legal context.
- Alberta — the Indigenous Primary Health Care Advisory Panel, its 22 recommendations and publicly documented implementation mechanisms.

Only evidence-backed dimensions are included. Reported experiences, formal findings, recommendations, government commitments, implementation and outcomes remain distinct.

## British Columbia — In Plain Sight

The 2020 review documented systemic Indigenous-specific racism and discrimination in B.C. health care and issued 24 recommendations. The current public-evidence ledger classifies two as implemented, three as substantially implemented, twelve as partially implemented, four as underway and three as supported by fragmentary implementation evidence.

Examples of concrete implementation include health-authority Public Interest Disclosure Act coverage, professional-regulation reform, a First Nations-led cultural-safety standard, complaint-process work and senior governance structures. The current Declaration Act page acknowledges that its reporting framework has limited ability to capture progress across all 24 recommendations and says a more comprehensive update is being explored.

A source discrepancy remains visible: current Action 3.07 reporting associates the disclosure-protection change with recommendation 2, while the original report and multiple earlier official sources assign it to recommendation 11. Miller North follows the original report's numbering and presents a neutral source note.

Later evidence added in this pass includes a June 2025 cross-government status report and BCCDC's December 2025 apology and leadership commitments.

## Saskatchewan — Saskatoon cohort

The Saskatoon Health Region-commissioned review documented the reported experiences of seven Indigenous women and interviewed nine providers. It issued ten Calls to Action covering rights-informed care, training, education, equal Indigenous governance partnership, workforce representation, coordinated support, reparation, reproductive support and consent policy.

Saskatchewan Health Authority states that its Indigenous Birth Support Worker Program was created as part of the response to the 2017 report. The program began in December 2019, was evaluated, and remains represented in current hospital service information. This is operational evidence for a concrete response; it does not demonstrate completion of all ten Calls to Action.

In *Popp v Canada (Attorney General)*, 2025 SKKB 89, the class-certification application was dismissed. The procedural decision did not adjudicate the merits of individual allegations. The 2026 Criminal Code amendment associated with Bill S-228 is shown as later national legal context, not a retroactive remedy.

## Alberta — Indigenous primary health care

Alberta's Indigenous Primary Health Care Advisory Panel produced 22 recommendations across five themes. Public sources describe a dedicated implementation plan, an Indigenous Health Division, navigator and innovation funding, advisory structures and an Indigenous Patient Safety Investigator and Advocate.

The advocate provides complaint support, advice and delegated Health Charter review, with explicit limits: the role cannot act as a court, reverse clinical decisions, discipline a provider or impose penalties. These boundaries are displayed alongside the mechanism.

Red Deer and Central Alberta were searched first. The search found cultural-safety concerns and operational Indigenous hospital-support services, but not a comparably deep local public-accountability chain. The provincewide panel was selected for the initial experience because its recommendations, government response, funding and operational mechanisms are better documented. This is not a finding that local problems are absent.

## What the comparison shows

- B.C. is primarily a systemic-review and distributed implementation-reporting case.
- Saskatchewan combines anonymized cohort evidence, an external review, a concrete service response and court procedure.
- Alberta is an advisory-panel and administrative-implementation case rather than an adjudicated incident case.
- Indigenous organizations and leaders have materially different governance, standards, review and service-delivery roles in each case.
- Funding, governance authority, policy adoption and operational implementation must not be collapsed into one claim.

## Evidence and presentation

Every material statement displayed in the new experience carries at least one public source. Sources are grouped as original reports, government records, health-system material, Indigenous-organization sources, oversight/regulatory sources, legal records and implementation/follow-up evidence. The browser projection excludes owner-review reasons, internal identifiers, candidate notes and anonymized-person details.

## Limitations

Evidence status describes available public reporting; it is not a rating of effectiveness, legal compliance or institutional performance. Missing public evidence does not establish that an action did not occur. Program creation does not establish provincewide operation, reach or positive outcomes. Legal sources are public-record context and not legal advice.

## Recommended next step

Before any public-launch decision, ask an Indigenous research or health-governance partner to review the framing, terminology, source balance and recommendation-status explanations in the actual interface.
