# Alberta Indigenous Patient Safety Investigator and Advocate — dossier review

Private owner artifact. Not a browser projection.

## Decision

`dossier_ready_with_gaps`

The case is mature enough for Research & Policy because authoritative public sources establish its origin, institutional location, named office-holder, operating access route, delegated authority, restrictions, and later public implementation activity. The dossier explicitly withholds claims of structural or investigative independence.

## Strongest findings

1. The Indigenous Primary Health Care Advisory Panel recommended a distinct complaints and investigation response as part of its first recommendation.
2. Alberta created the role within the Office of Alberta Health Advocates. The public job description places it in Alberta Health and reports to a Manager, Investigations.
3. Tony Cardinal began the role in October 2024. Government and First Nations Telehealth Network material confirms an operating intake, navigation, referral and follow-up function.
4. Delegated review authority applies where no other dispute-resolution process exists and conduct may be inconsistent with the Alberta Health Charter.
5. The role cannot reverse decisions, discipline providers, impose penalties, represent people in court or investigate several non-provincial/non-health bodies.

## Independence and governance finding

The evidence supports a government-created and ministry-situated role with delegated review authority and a mandate to build relationships with First Nations, Métis and Inuit representatives, Elders and Knowledge Keepers. It does not establish structural independence, an Indigenous governing board, Indigenous oversight authority or published co-governance terms. “Independent” is therefore not used as a public descriptor.

## Outcome evidence

Operational evidence exists. No comprehensive public outcome reporting was located for role-specific complaint volume, investigations, recommendations, remedies, referrals or resulting service/policy changes. This is a public-reporting limitation, not evidence of inactivity.

## Indigenous-led source coverage

Siksika Health Services preserved the 2023 announcement, and the First Nations Telehealth Network published a June 2026 learning session describing what patients and families can expect. These are meaningful Indigenous health / First Nations-serving implementation sources. No independent Indigenous-led evaluation of accessibility, trust, governance or effectiveness was located.

## Highest-value next question

Will the Office of Alberta Health Advocates publish a role-specific, privacy-protecting account of complaints, actions, recommendations and resulting changes?
