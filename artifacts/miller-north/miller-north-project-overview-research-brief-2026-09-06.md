## Executive summary

Miller North is a public-source research project about documented harmful, discriminatory, inequitable, or culturally unsafe Indigenous healthcare experiences and the institutional responses that followed. Its public experience now separates three stages of evidence maturity: recent signals under review, more developed evidence records, and deeper accountability or policy research.

## Three connected layers

### Live Listening

Live Listening shows recent or newly identified public reports that have passed a publication-safety check but may not yet support a developed incident record. Items are anonymized, source-linked, and labelled as reported, corroborated, linked to an existing record, or still under review.

Appearance in Live Listening does not mean an allegation was proven or that the item will become a canonical incident.

### Evidence Library

The Evidence Library presents approved public-source evidence. Multiple source-derived rows can be reconciled into one presentation group when deterministic evidence shows that displaying them separately would duplicate the same source context or bounded evidence statement.

The underlying records remain intact. Shared facilities or topics do not cause incidents to be merged, and ambiguous duplicate candidates remain separate for review.

### Research & Policy

Research & Policy follows selected cases beyond the original problem into investigations, recommendations, government or health-system responses, law and policy, implementation evidence, and later reporting. Initial cases cover In Plain Sight in British Columbia, the Saskatoon coerced sterilization/tubal-ligation cohort, and Alberta's Indigenous Primary Health Care Advisory Panel.

## How material moves

The intended pathway is:

public report → Live Listening → source verification and incident reconciliation → Evidence Library or systemic context → accountability and policy linkage → Research & Policy

Movement is not automatic. A signal may instead be withheld, rejected as insufficient, retained as contextual evidence, or attached as another source for an existing record.

## Evidence safeguards

- Public accounts remain distinct from formal findings.
- Source publication dates are not presented as event dates.
- Evidence-status labels describe source support, not moral or legal judgment.
- Missing public evidence does not prove that an event or action did not occur.
- Recommendation implementation statuses describe public evidence, not effectiveness or legal compliance.
- Legal material provides public-record context and is not legal advice.
- Miller North does not try to identify anonymized patients.
- Ordinary usernames, private notes, owner-review reasoning, and unnecessary personal details are excluded from browser projections.

## Current scale

- 695 approved source-derived Evidence Library rows
- 321 non-destructive public evidence groups
- 111 incident-like presentation groups
- 15 anonymized Live Listening items from 22 inspected leads
- 3 developed provincial Research & Policy cases
- 24 tracked In Plain Sight recommendations

These counts describe different research objects and should not be added together or interpreted as prevalence.

## Source balance

The current source base includes government, health-system, regulator, research, journalism, Indigenous-led/governance, and legal material. Indigenous-led sources are treated as primary evidence when they document governance decisions, standards, programs, formal concerns, or accountability work.

Current gaps include limited direct tribunal/human-rights source coverage in the Evidence Library, a broad unclassified organizational-source family, and uneven Indigenous-led implementation evaluation across provinces.

## Bounded post-deployment research update

### British Columbia

The strongest new B.C. evidence is a current Ministry of Health review of internal patient-safety and patient-initiated quality-review processes. Its scope expressly includes Indigenous-specific racism and discrimination, and the public engagement record says the work is intended to inform policy direction and possible legislative change. Separately, Island Health paused its acute-care Indigenous self-identification program after First Nations feedback and committed to distinctions-based redesign, shared governance, and Indigenous data-governance safeguards. These are separate stages: feedback, pause, redesign commitment, and future implementation. [Patient Care Quality Review](https://engage.gov.bc.ca/govtogetherbc/engagement/patient-care-quality-review/); [Island Health program pause](https://medicalstaff.islandhealth.ca/news-events/paused-indgi-self-identi)

B.C.'s 2026–28 Anti-Racism Action Plan also announces a Ministry of Health centre for anti-racism and cultural safety and schedules the first progress report for September 2027. The plan is evidence of a commitment, not yet proof that the centre is operational. [B.C. Anti-Racism Action Plan announcement](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026AG0032-000635)

### Saskatchewan

The First Nations Health Ombudsperson Office's inaugural report, covering July 2023 through March 2025, is a significant Indigenous-led accountability source. Legislative debate records the Health Minister saying the Ministry and Saskatchewan Health Authority were reviewing its recommendations. A later formal response was not located in this bounded pass. Federal UN Declaration reporting also confirms a Saskatchewan Anti-Indigenous Racism Navigator Network for health-system navigators from First Nations organizations, but does not name participants or publish outcome evidence. [FNHO annual reports](https://fnhoo.ca/annual-reports/); [Saskatchewan Hansard](https://docs.legassembly.sk.ca/legdocs/Assembly/Debates/30L2S/20251028DebatesHTML.htm); [Federal 2026 Action Plan reporting](https://justice.canada.ca/eng/declaration/report-rapport/2026/b1.html)

This evidence supports a new private FNHO accountability-chain candidate. It is not merged into the Saskatoon coerced-sterilization cohort because the public record does not establish a case-specific relationship.

### Alberta

Current Alberta reporting continues to document the Indigenous Health Division, navigator and innovation funding, an Indigenous Advisory Council, and the Indigenous Patient Safety Investigator and Advocate. The broader MAPS two-year-plan page reports 13 of 30 actions completed and 17 in progress, but those aggregate figures are not a recommendation-level ledger for the Indigenous panel's 22 recommendations. Alberta also reports 11 in-person and 27 virtual anti-racism engagement sessions intended to inform a What We Heard report and strategy; the final strategy was not located. [Reconciliation in Alberta](https://www.alberta.ca/reconciliation-in-alberta); [MAPS progress](https://www.alberta.ca/modernizing-primary-health-care-progress-on-2-year-plan); [Addressing racism in Indigenous health care](https://www.alberta.ca/addressing-racism-in-indigenous-health-care)

## Correction and accountability

The public methodology page describes a planned pathway for errors, source suggestions, updated evidence, and correction/removal review. No form is enabled until the intake destination and privacy handling are reviewed. Readers are expressly asked not to provide medical records or private personal information.

## Limitations

Miller North is not exhaustive and does not determine prevalence. Public reporting can be incomplete or distributed across institutions. Repetition of a concern is a research signal, not proof that a reform failed. Program creation does not establish reach, consistent operation, effectiveness, or improved outcomes.

## Recommended next step

Conduct an Indigenous governance and editorial review of the now-deployed experience, with particular attention to the two new B.C. updates and the proposed FNHO accountability dossier, before promoting Miller North to a wider audience.
