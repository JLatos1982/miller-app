# Miller North three-province comparison

> Descriptive comparison of accountability structures, not harm severity.

| Dimension | British Columbia | Saskatchewan | Alberta |
|---|---|---|---|
| How concern became public | Independent systemic review commissioned amid widespread reports | Anonymized cohort accounts examined by a commissioned external review | Advisory-panel process plus earlier public engagement evidence |
| Main accountability form | 24-recommendation review and task-team implementation reporting | Ten Calls to Action, apology, program response and litigation | 22 advisory recommendations and a government implementation plan |
| Government role | Lead, legislator, funder and reporter | Health-system response plus later federal committee/law context | Panel sponsor, response author, funder and program administrator |
| Indigenous organization role | Governance partner, standards co-author and evaluation source | Review authorship, advocacy and recommended equal governance partnership | Panel leadership, design partner and funded recipient/service roles |
| Regulator/law role | Professional-regulation reform and PIDA coverage | Procedural litigation and later federal Criminal Code amendment | Health Charter review context; advocate has non-court, non-regulator limits |
| Implementation evidence | Broadest, but distributed across institutions | Strong for one birth-support service; weakly disaggregated for the full ten Calls | Strong for implementation machinery; incomplete 22-row reporting |
| Public reporting gap | No current comprehensive 24-row ledger located | No current comprehensive ten-action ledger located | No current comprehensive 22-row ledger located |
| Funding boundary | Program-level context not attributed to individual recommendations | Operational program found without a service-level amount | Program and recipient funding found; not equivalent to outcomes |

## Shared finding

All three cases benefit from the same evidence path—problem or advice → recommendation → response → implementation mechanism → later evidence—but the entry point and authority differ. B.C. is review-and-report led; Saskatchewan combines cohort review, service response and court process; Alberta is advisory-panel and administrative-implementation led.

## Recurrent gap

Recommendation-level public reporting becomes less complete over time. That is a source-coverage finding, not evidence that institutions did not act.
