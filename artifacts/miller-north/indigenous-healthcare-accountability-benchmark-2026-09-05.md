# Indigenous Healthcare Accountability Benchmark

Read-only research benchmark for Miller North owner review, generated 2026-09-05. This report does not alter Miller, Miller North, Supabase, or any production record. The companion [structured candidate dataset](indigenous-healthcare-accountability-benchmark-2026-09-05.json) is the authoritative record-level artifact.

## Scope and method

The benchmark covers British Columbia, Alberta, and Saskatchewan. It prioritizes official reviews, health-authority publications, governments, regulators, human-rights bodies, Indigenous health organizations, and court or legal-process reporting. Records are deduplicated by accountability action, not article: a launch, final report, apology, regulation, implementation update, and unresolved legal process can be separate stages of one `accountability_chain_id`.

The dataset deliberately distinguishes an allegation from a finding; a recommendation from an accepted commitment; and an announcement from verified implementation. “Partly implemented,” “underway,” and “planned” do not mean completed.

## Headline result

37 distinct accountability records met the benchmark threshold: 13 in B.C., 12 in Alberta, and 12 in Saskatchewan. Of these, 27 use an official primary source, five use official plus independent corroboration, four use independent corroboration, and one is reported but unconfirmed. Six require owner review before any downstream use.

### Recommended future data model (not implemented)

Add an `accountability_actions` entity linked many-to-many with `miller_north_incidents`, rather than putting all accountability detail into `incident_summary` or an unstructured source role. High-value fields are:

- `accountability_action_id`, `accountability_chain_id`, `incident_id`, `parent_action_id`
- `action_type`, `stage` (`launched`, `finding`, `recommendation`, `commitment`, `implemented`, `progress_update`, `unresolved`)
- `triggering_issue`, `finding_status`, `implementation_status`, `implementation_evidence_date`
- `organization`, `jurisdiction`, `complaint_or_case_reference` only when public and necessary
- `recommendations`, `response`, `owner_review_flag`, `owner_review_reason`
- action-level source records with primary/corroborating role and independent-source flag.

Useful relationships are `incident -> complaint`, `complaint -> investigation`, `investigation -> report`, `report -> recommendation`, `recommendation -> commitment`, `commitment -> implementation evidence`, and `action -> progress report`. This structure prevents an announced training program from being mistaken for a completed corrective action.

### Productive sources and search expansion

Most productive source families were provincial health-ministry news and engagement sites; health-authority annual reports and strategy pages; regulator action plans; human-rights commission case summaries; Indigenous-led ombudsperson/health-organization publications; and commissioned review PDFs. General news was most valuable for discovering unresolved legal processes and then locating official follow-up.

Useful added discovery terms include:

- `"Indigenous Patient Safety Investigator"`, `"First Nations Health Ombudsperson"`, `"patient care quality review"`
- `"external review" "tubal ligation"`, `"cultural responsiveness training"`, `"Indigenous-specific anti-racism framework"`
- `"equity audit" hospital`, `"directed mediation" hospital racism`, `"quality assurance review" Indigenous hospital`
- `"protective services" hospital Indigenous`, `"patient navigator" Indigenous health`, `"implementation status" In Plain Sight`.
