## Why it matters

Miller North now has a deployed, human-readable path from public reports to grouped evidence and then to institutional response. The new research also demonstrates that current official sources can add meaningful stages without turning announcements into proof of implementation.

## Strongest findings

1. B.C.'s 2026 patient-care-quality review explicitly includes Indigenous-specific racism and may inform policy and legislative change.
2. Island Health's pause and redesign of Indigenous self-identification separates First Nations feedback, program pause, governance redesign, and future implementation.
3. The FNHO inaugural report creates a strong Saskatchewan Indigenous-led accountability chain; the provincial response currently stops at a documented review commitment.
4. Federal reporting confirms a Saskatchewan Anti-Indigenous Racism Navigator Network but does not provide participants or outcome evidence.
5. Alberta's aggregate MAPS progress reporting cannot safely be mapped to the 22 Indigenous-panel recommendations without a recommendation-level crosswalk.

## Uncertainty / caution

- A later formal Saskatchewan response to the FNHO inaugural report was not located.
- The complete FNHO report should be reviewed before extracting recommendations.
- B.C.'s new anti-racism centre is an announced commitment until operational evidence is published.
- Island Health's redesign timeline and resumption criteria are not yet public.
- Alberta's planned Indigenous Anti-Racism Strategy was not located in final form.

## Recommended next move

Run an Indigenous governance and editorial review of the deployed experience, then authorize a bounded FNHO dossier build if the full inaugural report confirms a sufficiently structured recommendation and response chain.
