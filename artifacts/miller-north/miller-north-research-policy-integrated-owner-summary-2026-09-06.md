# Miller North Research & Policy — integrated owner summary

> Private owner-review artifact. The hidden-site projection contains only publication-safe public-source material.

## What changed

- Added a new **Research & Policy** destination beside the existing Evidence Library.
- Added a calm landing page explaining how Miller North follows a concern through review, recommendation, institutional response, implementation and later evidence.
- Added complete mobile-friendly views for In Plain Sight, the Saskatoon coerced-sterilization cohort and Alberta's Indigenous Primary Health Care Advisory Panel.
- Added the 24-row In Plain Sight recommendation tracker with theme and evidence-status filters, expandable evidence stories and source links.
- Added readable timelines, accountability paths, policy/legal context, evidence-backed “what we know” and “what remains unclear” sections, and grouped source libraries.

## Why it matters

Miller North can now show what followed a documented problem without asking readers to understand evidence graphs, migrations or Farm terminology. It can also show the boundary between a recommendation, an announced response, an operational mechanism and measured outcomes.

## Strongest case

**In Plain Sight** remains the strongest demonstration. It has a canonical 24-recommendation baseline, a later task-team report, distributed current implementation evidence, eight understandable themes and a transparent public-reporting limitation.

## New discoveries

- **British Columbia:** a June 2025 cross-government update adds later patient-feedback and Métis health-table evidence; BCCDC issued an apology and leadership commitments in December 2025 connected to In Plain Sight.
- **Alberta:** current provincial reporting now presents the Indigenous Health Division, navigator and innovation funding, advisory council and patient-safety advocate as continuing response mechanisms; the navigator program lists a 2026–2029 funding round.
- **Saskatchewan:** current searching reinforced the operational birth-support evidence and 2025 certification decision but did not locate a new ten-action status ledger or appeal decision.
- No new candidate case met the threshold for main navigation.

## What remains weak

- None of the three provinces provides one current public recommendation-by-recommendation status and outcome ledger for the selected case.
- Program and office creation are easier to verify than consistent reach, operation and outcomes.
- Saskatchewan consent-policy follow-up and any appeal after the 2025 certification decision remain unresolved publicly.
- Alberta's 22 recommendations still need a detailed public-evidence crosswalk.
- The experience has source-level tests but not yet automated screenshot or browser-interaction coverage.

## Recommended next move

Conduct one accessibility and content review with an Indigenous research or health-governance partner before treating the hidden experience as ready for a public launch decision.
