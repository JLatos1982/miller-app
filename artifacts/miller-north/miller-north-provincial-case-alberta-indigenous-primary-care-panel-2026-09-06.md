# Alberta Indigenous Primary Health Care Advisory Panel and implementation

> Private owner-review dossier. No publication or production mutation is authorized.

## What happened

Alberta convened an Indigenous Primary Health Care Advisory Panel that produced 22 recommendations for culturally safe, Indigenous-led and accountable primary health care. Government then published an implementation plan and created or funded several mechanisms, including an Indigenous Health Division, navigator grants, an innovation fund and an Indigenous Patient Safety Investigator and Advocate.

## Why this matters

- It is Alberta's strongest defensible public chain from Indigenous-led advice to government commitments and operational mechanisms.
- It shows why a program, funding stream and complaints office should remain separate evidence stages.
- The Red Deer search still yielded useful local context, while avoiding a forced weak local case.

## The one thing to remember

Alberta publicly demonstrates implementation machinery, but not yet one complete public account of the status and outcomes of all 22 recommendations.

## Review first

- Multiple implementation mechanisms are verifiable, but no current comprehensive public status ledger for all 22 recommendations was located.
- The Indigenous Patient Safety Investigator and Advocate is operational with defined powers and limits; it is not a court, professional regulator or substitute for every complaint route.
- Navigator and innovation funding are implementation evidence, not evidence of provincewide access or outcomes.

## Timeline

- 2022-09-29 — **Red Deer engagement identifies cultural-safety concerns.** Participants raised cultural safety, racism and bias as health-system concerns; this was consultation evidence, not a formal case finding.
- 2022-2023 — **Indigenous advisory panel convened.** Indigenous leaders and health-system participants developed recommendations for primary health-care reform.
- 2023 — **Panel issues 22 recommendations.** The final report organizes recommendations around culturally safe, Indigenous-led and accountable care.
- 2023 — **Early investment opportunities published.** Government identifies early commitments and implementation mechanisms.
- 2024 — **The Way Forward published.** A separate Indigenous primary-health-care plan sets out implementation work.
- 2024-2026 — **Funding and oversight mechanisms operate.** Navigator grants, an innovation fund, an Indigenous Health Division and patient-safety advocate are publicly documented.
- 2024-10 to 2025-07 — **Anti-racism engagement held.** Government gathered input and reported that results were under review; a final resulting strategy was not located.

## Accountability chain

- indigenous_primary_care_panel → **recommends** → panel_22_recommendations: The panel report issues 22 recommendations.
- the_way_forward → **responds_to** → panel_22_recommendations: The implementation plan is a formal government response.
- indigenous_health_division → **responds_to** → panel_22_recommendations: Government reports the division as an implementation structure.
- panel_22_recommendations → **partially_implements** → indigenous_patient_navigator_grants: Navigator grants operationalize part of access and navigation recommendations.
- panel_22_recommendations → **funded_by** → indigenous_primary_care_innovation_fund: The fund supports implementation projects; program-level funding is not service-level attribution.
- indigenous_patient_safety_advocate → **responds_to** → panel_22_recommendations: The advocate is a current oversight and complaint-support mechanism.
- indigenous_patient_safety_advocate → **governed_by** → alberta_health_charter: The advocate may undertake delegated Health Charter reviews within defined limits.
- anti_racism_engagement_2024 → **reports_on** → indigenous_healthcare_racism_concerns: Later engagement documents continuing policy attention without proving earlier reform failure.
- rdrhc_indigenous_support → **has_context** → central_alberta_service_environment: The service establishes local operational context but is not attributed to the panel without evidence.

## Who is involved

- Alberta Health — policy lead, response author and funder
- Indigenous Primary Health Care Advisory Panel — recommendation body with Indigenous and health-system participants
- Office of Alberta Health Advocates — home of the Indigenous Patient Safety Investigator and Advocate
- Alberta Health Services — health-service operator and Red Deer/Central context source
- Indigenous organizations and funded recipients — distinct governance and service-delivery partners

## Government / policy / law

- The Way Forward implementation plan (directly_applicable): The plan is the direct government implementation framework for panel recommendations.
- Alberta Health Charter (directly_applicable): The advocate can conduct delegated Health Charter reviews; the Charter guides expectations but is not treated here as adjudicating individual legal liability.
- Treaty 6, 7 and 8 and Métis context (governance_context): The panel report itself frames Indigenous participation and distinctions; no additional treaty legal consequence is inferred.

## What changed

- policy_adoption: The Way Forward is a formal response plan tied to the panel recommendations.
- funding: Navigator and innovation funding mechanisms are publicly documented.
- office_or_program_creation: The Indigenous Health Division and Indigenous Patient Safety Investigator and Advocate are documented structures.
- operational_implementation: The advocate provides a current complaint-support and system-advice mechanism with specified powers and limits.
- reporting: Current government pages list completed and underway actions, but not a complete current 22-row status ledger.

## What we know

- The panel report contains 22 recommendations.
- Government published a separate Indigenous implementation plan.
- Current pages document funding and oversight mechanisms.
- The patient-safety advocate has a Health Charter review and advisory role with explicit limits.

## What remains uncertain

- Current status of each of the 22 recommendations.
- Geographic distribution and sustained operation of all funded initiatives.
- Outcome evidence for navigator, innovation and complaint mechanisms.
- Whether the anti-racism engagement produced a final strategy after the last public update.
