# Treaty and Indigenous Legal Research Foundation

Date: 2026-09-06
Scope: design only; no Treaty 6 crawl and no legal advice

## Purpose

Extend the Legal & Government Research Branch so a later Miller North benchmark can document treaty, rights, jurisdiction, health-governance, and agreement context without flattening distinct Nations or asserting legal conclusions.

## Object classes

- historic and modern treaties;
- self-government and sectoral governance agreements;
- federal/provincial statutes and regulations;
- health-transfer and service-delivery agreements;
- court and tribunal decisions;
- inquiry, commission, and implementation reports;
- Nation, tribal council, First Nations health organization, federal, provincial, and health-authority governance actors.

## Research questions

1. Which instrument is authoritative, which parties are named, and what geographic or community scope is explicit?
2. What health responsibilities, authorities, funding arrangements, or implementation mechanisms are stated?
3. Is the material binding, advisory, proposed, historical, amended, or superseded?
4. Which government or First Nations body has the documented role?
5. Does the instrument connect directly to a known incident, cohort, accountability action, commitment, service, or implementation claim?
6. What later agreement, law, decision, or report changes or evaluates the arrangement?
7. What is unresolved or contested and therefore requires expert/owner review?

## Priority source families

Crown-Indigenous Relations treaty/agreement sources; Indigenous Services Canada; Justice Laws; Supreme Court and appellate court decisions; provincial legislation; official treaty commissions; First Nations governments and organizations; First Nations Health Council and FNHA; provincial ministries; human-rights bodies; public inquiries and commissions; Auditor General and ombudsperson reports.

## Required distinctions

- Treaty party, Nation, band, tribal council, health authority, and service operator are not interchangeable.
- Federal funding, provincial funding, First Nations governance, and delivery relationships are separate edges.
- A court holding, party submission, government characterization, and scholarly interpretation are separate source roles.
- Territory or treaty-area overlap is not by itself evidence that an incident is legally governed by a particular provision.

## Later Treaty 6 benchmark design

Begin with a bounded source registry and a small set of authoritative instruments. Build party/jurisdiction timelines, then connect only evidence-backed health-governance relationships. Require owner review for contested interpretation, overlapping jurisdiction, and any claim that a treaty provision determines a healthcare obligation.
