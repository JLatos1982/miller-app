# Miller North owner summary — product depth and Watching Now

Private owner review · 2026-09-06

## What changed

- The Evidence Library now opens with a four-choice “Start here” guide.
- All four mature Research & Policy cases now answer what the case is, why it matters, what changed, and what remains unclear before presenting technical evidence.
- A descriptive three-province comparison explains different accountability structures without ranking provinces or harms.
- “Watching Now” adds eight publication-safe developing stories: three in B.C., three in Alberta and two in Saskatchewan.
- The research lifecycle is explicit: lead → emerging case → dossier candidate → Research & Policy case. Advancement is not automatic.

## Private-candidate review

The 20-record private working set was inspected against the existing publication-safe Live Listening layer. Fifteen source URLs are already represented as anonymized Live Listening items. None was promoted from an incident-like signal to an institutional Emerging Case solely because a public name or news report exists.

The Yvonne Houssin and Nathan Cushman source accounts remain suitable only for the anonymized Live Listening treatment already in place. In this pass, no sufficiently deep later public accountability chain was located to justify a Watching Now card. Five other working candidates remain held or owner-review gated. No private row, evidence excerpt or identity field entered the browser dataset.

## Strongest developments

1. **B.C. Patient Care Quality Review.** The Ministry’s 2026 review explicitly includes Indigenous-specific racism and discrimination and may inform policy direction and legislative change.
2. **Island Health self-identification redesign.** Public evidence supports distinct stages—community feedback, program pause and redesign commitment—but not yet completed redesign or resumption.
3. **Alberta Indigenous Patient Safety Investigator and Advocate.** This is the closest current dossier candidate because an operating accountability mechanism and delegated review role are publicly described.
4. **Alberta anti-racism strategy reporting gap.** Thirty-eight engagement sessions and promised outputs are public; a final What We Heard report or strategy was not located in the bounded official search.
5. **Saskatchewan Navigator Network.** Federal reporting confirms a network purpose for navigators from First Nations organizations, while participants, governance and outcomes remain publicly unclear.

## Legal, human-rights and Indigenous-led source balance

Direct legal context improved through provincial human-rights materials and Alberta’s Health Charter-linked advocate mechanism. Case-level tribunal decisions remain thin. Indigenous-led and Indigenous-governed sources are strongest in FNHO and the Siksika public complaint source; institutional implementation reporting still comes mainly from government and health-system pages. This is a perspective gap, not a call for artificial numerical balance.

## What remains weak

- No current public recommendation crosswalk for Alberta’s 22 panel recommendations.
- No comprehensive later Saskatchewan/SHA response ledger for FNHO recommendations.
- No final Alberta Indigenous health anti-racism report located.
- No Island Health redesign milestones beyond the pause and commitment.
- Direct adjudicative human-rights decisions tied to the current cases remain scarce.

## Recommended next move

Wait for and capture the first substantive output from the B.C. Patient Care Quality Review or Alberta Patient Safety Investigator. Either could support the next mature dossier without lowering the evidence threshold.
