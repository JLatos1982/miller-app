# Procurement Usefulness Assessment

## Finding

Procurement records can materially help when a solicitation or award names an addiction-service deliverable, supplier, value and term. They do not prove that the service opened, remained available, met capacity targets or achieved outcomes.

## Pilot observations

- BC Bid permits public browsing of opportunities, awards and bid results and is used by health authorities and broader public-sector organizations.
- Historical B.C. contract disclosure identifies a Providence Health Care agreement for evidence-based information and guidance on substance-use-disorder care, amended to $17.85 million through March 2023. This is useful institutional/program backstory, not a Miller treatment-resource allocation.
- The bounded search did not locate an award record that safely attributes the Inlet Recovery Home amount to Connective. The official service announcement proves the operator relationship, not the contract value.
- Supplier-payment schedules can establish that money moved to an organization, but the payee name alone may not establish purpose, contract, service or location.

## Recommended use

Search procurement only after a policy, funding or service chain identifies a likely buyer, supplier, program and time window. Require a separate operational source before linking a contract to an operating Miller resource.

## Non-goals

Do not create vendor surveillance, rank contractors, or infer procurement irregularity from sole-source status, amendments, missing search results or a mismatch between contract maximum and expenditures.

## Registry change

Added `fogs_bc_bid` for opportunities, awards, notices of intent, results and amendments.
