# Research Brief — Saskatchewan First Nations Health Ombudsperson Office — governance and funding

> Private draft for owner review.

## Executive summary

The Saskatchewan First Nations Health Ombudsperson Office is a First Nations-designed and governed health-oversight mechanism. This dossier separates its Chiefs-in-Assembly governance authority from federal contribution funding and from evidence that the office became and remains operational. This is the strongest cross-domain fixture for proving that Farm relationships can preserve who governs, who funds, who operates and what evidence demonstrates implementation without collapsing those roles.

## Scope

This brief assembles existing private Miller North Indigenous healthcare accountability evidence for Saskatchewan First Nations Health Ombudsperson Office — governance and funding. It does not make a publication decision, legal conclusion, funding-performance judgment or causal claim.

## Timeline

- 2017: Chiefs-in-Assembly resolution establishes mandate — The office identifies FSIN Chiefs-in-Assembly Resolution 2046 as the basis for its First Nations governance authority.
- 2022-02-22: $1.17-million first-year establishment funding announced — Indigenous Services Canada announced contribution funding for FSIN to establish the office.
- 2024-05-10 to 2026-03-31: $775,000 agreement disclosed — Federal proactive disclosure identifies the recipient, value and term but not detailed activity-level spending.
- current as inspected 2026-09-06: Office and annual reporting remain available — The FNHO site describes current operation and provides an annual-report registry.

## Findings

- The office identifies FSIN Chiefs-in-Assembly Resolution 2046 as the source of its governance authority.
- Indigenous Services Canada announced $1.17 million for first-year establishment, but funding is not the source of the office’s First Nations governance mandate.
- Current office information and annual-report access demonstrate operation beyond the launch announcement.
- Federal proactive disclosure identifies a later $775,000 contribution agreement, while detailed activity-level allocation remains outside the disclosed record.
- The office describes Treaty and inherent-rights context; this dossier does not convert that governance statement into a judicial treaty-right conclusion.

## Evidence

- The office identifies Chiefs-in-Assembly Resolution 2046 as its governance authority. [fdsource_fnho_governance]
- The office frames its mission in the spirit and intent of Treaty and inherent rights; no judicial treaty-right determination is inferred. [fdsource_fnho_governance]
- Indigenous Services Canada announced $1.17 million for first-year establishment through FSIN. [fdsource_fnho_isc_launch]
- Current office information and annual-report access demonstrate operation after establishment funding. [fdsource_fnho_isc_launch, fdsource_fnho_about, fdsource_fnho_annual_reports]
- Federal proactive disclosure confirms a later $775,000 agreement at recipient level without itemized activity spending. [fdsource_fnho_open_grant]
- The office maintains an annual-report registry for continuing accountability follow-up. [fdsource_fnho_annual_reports]

## Limitations

The dossier demonstrates governance authority, named federal contributions and operation. It does not determine legal treaty rights, audit expenditure, expose individual complaint information or attribute federal funding to particular complaint outcomes.

## Unresolved questions

- What complaint, resolution, systemic-recommendation and outcome measures are reported in the latest FNHO annual report?
- How was the $775,000 contribution agreement allocated among office activities?
