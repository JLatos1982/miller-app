# Research Brief — Creekside Withdrawal Management Centre — Road to Recovery expansion

> Private draft for owner review.

## Executive summary

Creekside Withdrawal Management Centre is a current Fraser Health service and a named implementation site within British Columbia’s Road to Recovery expansion. This dossier connects the provincewide commitment to a ten-bed local expansion, current service identity and later utilization reporting. This case shows how Miller can connect a broad policy-and-funding commitment to a verified local resource without incorrectly converting a program envelope into a site-level amount.

## Scope

This brief assembles existing private Miller addictions evidence for Creekside Withdrawal Management Centre — Road to Recovery expansion. It does not make a publication decision, legal conclusion, funding-performance judgment or causal claim.

## Timeline

- 2023-10-11: Road to Recovery launched in Vancouver — The Province described a standardized access-and-treatment model supported by $23.7 million in annual program funding.
- 2024-07-22: Provincewide Road to Recovery expansion announced — The Province announced nearly $154 million over three years for 100 beds, outpatient services and regional access lines.
- 2024-04-01 to 2025-03-31: Road to Recovery utilization reported — The Ministry’s annual report provides call and bed-access measures for the wider model, not a Creekside-only outcome assessment.
- 2026-02-03: Ten added Creekside beds reported operational — Fraser Health named Creekside, the added bed count and related implementation supports.

## Findings

- The Province announced nearly $154 million over three years for the provincewide Road to Recovery expansion, including 100 beds and regional access pathways.
- Fraser Health later identified Creekside as the site of ten additional withdrawal-management beds, with staffing and transportation support.
- The current Fraser Health directory corroborates Creekside’s continued operation and its Miller curated-resource identity.
- Ministry reporting provides broader Road to Recovery utilization evidence, but the public sources do not allocate a share of the provincewide envelope to Creekside.

## Limitations

The dossier can verify a provincewide commitment, a named Creekside expansion and continued operation. It cannot assign a portion of the wider funding envelope to Creekside or use wider-program utilization as a site-level effectiveness measure.

## Unresolved questions

- What share, if any, of the nearly $154-million Road to Recovery envelope was allocated to the Creekside expansion?
- Are Creekside-specific post-expansion utilization or capacity measures publicly reported?
