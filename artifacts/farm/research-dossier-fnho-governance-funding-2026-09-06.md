# Private Research Dossier — Saskatchewan First Nations Health Ombudsperson Office — governance and funding

> Private owner-review artifact. Not a publication decision.

## Owner synthesis

### What this is

The Saskatchewan First Nations Health Ombudsperson Office is a First Nations-designed and governed health-oversight mechanism. This dossier separates its Chiefs-in-Assembly governance authority from federal contribution funding and from evidence that the office became and remains operational.

### What we learned

- The office identifies FSIN Chiefs-in-Assembly Resolution 2046 as the source of its governance authority.
- Indigenous Services Canada announced $1.17 million for first-year establishment, but funding is not the source of the office’s First Nations governance mandate.
- Current office information and annual-report access demonstrate operation beyond the launch announcement.
- Federal proactive disclosure identifies a later $775,000 contribution agreement, while detailed activity-level allocation remains outside the disclosed record.
- The office describes Treaty and inherent-rights context; this dossier does not convert that governance statement into a judicial treaty-right conclusion.

### Why it matters

This is the strongest cross-domain fixture for proving that Farm relationships can preserve who governs, who funds, who operates and what evidence demonstrates implementation without collapsing those roles.

### What remains unclear

- What activity-level outputs and outcomes are reported in the latest FNHO annual report?
- How was the later $775,000 agreement allocated among office activities?

### Recommended next move

Review the latest annual report at activity level and keep all future summaries explicit that First Nations governance authority and federal funding are distinct relationships.
