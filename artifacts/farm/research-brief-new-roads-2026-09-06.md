# Research Brief — New Roads Therapeutic Recovery Community

> Private draft for owner review.

## Executive summary

New Roads Therapeutic Recovery Community is an operating, nonprofit-run residential recovery program in the Victoria area. This dossier connects a named provincial funding commitment to its recipient, continuing service evidence and a later women’s-program expansion. This is the clearest canonical example of public money moving from a named government announcement to a named operator and an observable service, while preserving a remaining resource-identity decision.

## Scope

This brief assembles existing private Miller addictions evidence for New Roads Therapeutic Recovery Community. It does not make a publication decision, legal conclusion, funding-performance judgment or causal claim.

## Timeline

- 2023-10-17: $4.39-million three-year support announced — The Province named New Roads and estimated the support would reach 330 people over three years.
- current as inspected 2026-09-06: Island Health lists New Roads — The health-authority service directory identifies New Roads among current community bed-based treatment and recovery options.
- 2025-03-14: 20-bed women’s facility opens — An official opening notice documents a distinct 20-bed women’s therapeutic-recovery facility connected to New Roads.
- current as inspected 2026-09-06: Operator maintains current program information — Our Place Society’s New Roads site provides current program and access information.

## Findings

- The Province named New Roads and announced $4.39 million over three years to support an estimated 330 participants.
- Our Place Society is the named operator and recipient relationship; Island Health currently lists the program within its treatment-and-recovery directory.
- A separate 20-bed women’s facility opened in March 2025, demonstrating a later expansion of the New Roads model.
- The funding-to-service path reaches service level, but Miller still needs an owner decision about whether the men’s and women’s programs are one resource or related resources.

## Evidence

- The Province announced a named $4.39-million, three-year funding commitment for New Roads. [fdsource_new_roads_bc_funding]
- The official announcement associates the funding with the Our Place Society-operated New Roads program. [fdsource_new_roads_bc_funding]
- The operator and health-authority pages identify New Roads as a current service. [fdsource_new_roads_island_health, fdsource_new_roads_operator]
- Current service evidence supports a service-level funding-to-operation path; it does not provide detailed expenditure attribution. [fdsource_new_roads_bc_funding, fdsource_new_roads_island_health]
- An official opening notice documents the later 20-bed women’s facility as an expansion of the New Roads model. [fdsource_new_roads_women_opening]

## Limitations

The dossier establishes a named funding-to-operation path but does not audit expenditure, evaluate effectiveness or allocate costs among program components. It also leaves the final Miller resource granularity to owner review.

## Unresolved questions

- Should the men’s and women’s New Roads programs be represented as one Miller resource, two resources or a parent with two programs?
- Is there an official report comparing actual participation with the announced estimate of 330 people over three years?
