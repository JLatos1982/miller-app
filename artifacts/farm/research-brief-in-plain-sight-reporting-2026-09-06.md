# Research Brief — In Plain Sight — implementation and public-reporting accountability

> Private draft for owner review.

## Executive summary

In Plain Sight documented Indigenous-specific racism in British Columbia health care and issued 24 recommendations. This dossier assembles the formal response, recommendation-level implementation examples and the Province’s current limitation in reporting comprehensively across all 24 recommendations. The case tests whether Farm can represent documented progress and a material reporting gap at the same time without converting incomplete public reporting into a claim of institutional failure.

## Scope

This brief assembles existing private Miller North Indigenous healthcare accountability evidence for In Plain Sight — implementation and public-reporting accountability. It does not make a publication decision, legal conclusion, funding-performance judgment or causal claim.

## Timeline

- 2020-11-30: In Plain Sight final report and recommendations — The independent review documented findings and issued 24 recommendations concerning Indigenous-specific racism in B.C. health care.
- 2021-11-30: FNHA publishes one-year update — FNHA reported anti-racism, cultural-safety and humility work one year after In Plain Sight.
- 2023-11-30: Province issues 24-month update — The official update described implementation activity connected to the recommendations.
- 2024: Estimates notes document specific implementation examples — The official notes describe recommendation-level activity including governance, complaint, professional-standard, legislative and training work.
- current as inspected 2026-09-06: Action 3.07 reports progress and a coverage limitation — The current action page identifies implementation activity while stating that the framework does not comprehensively report all 24 recommendations.

## Findings

- Declaration Act Action 3.07 is the Province’s current formal implementation vehicle for the recommendations.
- Official sources document specific activity connected to recommendations 1, 2, 17, 22, 23 and 24, including governance, a complaint process, a standard, legislative reform and education tools.
- The current Action 3.07 page says the reporting framework does not yet capture comprehensive progress across all 24 recommendations.
- Later federal reporting identifies a $171.8-million Indigenous Health and Cultural Safety Fund connected to recommendation 17, but the amount is program-level and not attributable to the full recommendation set.
- The reporting limitation is a source-backed accountability finding; it is not evidence that all unreported recommendations were unimplemented.

## Limitations

This dossier relies on public institutional reporting and does not infer completion from activity, infer non-implementation from silence, provide legal advice or assess whether reforms caused particular outcomes. The $171.8-million amount is program-level context, not a recommendation-by-recommendation allocation.

## Unresolved questions

- Is there a current comprehensive official status ledger covering all 24 recommendations?
- What is the current status and evidence date for each of the 18 recommendations not disaggregated in the assembled current sources?
- Which implementation claims have independent operational or outcome evaluation?
