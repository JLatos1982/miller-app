# Owner Summary — Farm cross-domain operational pilot

## What changed

- The shared foundation ran across six Miller resources and seven Miller North chains without merging domain data.
- It validated 39 evidence edges, ranked 12 material findings and exercised 14 of 27 registered source families.
- Nine high-value source families were added to the private registry; 13 of the 14 used families produced meaningful evidence.

## Why it matters

The pilot proves that shared source, edge, privacy, significance and synthesis contracts can support two different public-interest domains while their records and public views remain separate.

## Strongest evidence

- A policy-to-service path can reach current operation and utilization, not merely an announcement.
- An Indigenous-governance path can preserve First Nations authority separately from federal funding.
- The same official source can support documented progress and a carefully scoped implementation limitation.

## Uncertainty / caution

- Eight selected objects remain owner-gated.
- Funding is usually public at program level, not resource level.
- Saskatchewan statute retrieval and grant-detail access were less reliable.
- The ranking is a review-priority score, never an effectiveness, compliance or reputational score.

## Recommended next move

Owner-review the In Plain Sight reporting limitation first, then approve one Miller backstory and the FNHO governance chain as canonical fixtures before widening the operational pilot.
