# Public Money Traceability Benchmark

Private owner-review artifact. Research date: 2026-09-06.

## Scope and result

The pilot traced 16 public-money records: ten Miller addictions records and six Miller North records. Fifteen contain an official numeric amount; one confirms administrative and operational implementation without locating a role-specific amount. The records carry 32 source-backed financial edges.

Trace outcomes:

- 4 service-level funding confirmations
- 2 recipient-level funding confirmations
- 4 program-level funding confirmations
- 1 operational-result confirmation at a network level
- 3 records where an amount cannot be attributed to a named service
- 1 announced project without later completion evidence
- 1 partial trace with no role-specific amount

Amounts were not summed because the dataset contains overlapping budget envelopes, program expansions, continuation funding and amended/extended federal projects.

## Strong example chains

### New Roads

B.C. announcement of $4.39 million over three years → Our Place Society/New Roads → current Island Health listing → later women's-program opening. This is the cleanest selected service-level provincial trace.

### Kílala Lelum

Health Canada SUAP register → $6,065,479 recipient/project amount → prescribed alternatives and Elder-led cultural-healing program. The funded-project register supports the named activity; it does not establish an effectiveness conclusion.

### FNHO

Chiefs-in-Assembly governance authority → $1.17-million federal first-year establishment support → operating First Nations Health Ombudsperson Office → later $775,000 contribution record. Governance authority, funding and operation remain distinct.

### Road to Recovery

$23.7 million annually for initial operation/monitoring and wider expansion, followed by a nearly $154-million three-year expansion envelope → named services and utilization. The service relationships are strong; site-level dollar attribution is not.

## Limits of attribution

Budget announcements are productive for program, purpose and intended scale. They usually do not provide recipient payment schedules or site allocations. Health-authority service pages prove operation, not how a provincial envelope was divided. Audited statements provide aggregate ministry contributions and expense categories, usually not named-service expenditure.

## Audit and later reporting

Later provincial implementation summaries and health-authority/FNHA reports help confirm beds, service use or institutional operation. They are not expenditure audits of each selected commitment. The pilot found no basis for claims about efficiency, adequacy, waste or value for money.

## Highest review needs

- reconcile overlapping B.C. Budget 2023, CMHA bed and Road to Recovery totals;
- retain First Nations governance separately from funder/transfer relationships;
- confirm delivery of the funded St. Paul's cultural-safety curriculum;
- do not infer Alberta advocate funding from office-level totals;
- treat federal project amendments/extensions as related stages, not automatically additive amounts.
