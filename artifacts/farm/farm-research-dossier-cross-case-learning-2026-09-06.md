# Cross-Dossier Learning Memo

## What generalized

The same evidence-path pattern worked in both domains:

`formal response or program → commitment/funding/governance step → operational evidence → follow-up or reporting boundary`

Recurring relationship families were `responds_to`, `funded_by`, `received_by`, `created_by`, `operationalizes`, `implements`, `evaluated_by` and `reported_by`. The objects differed by domain, but the evidence-bearing edge contract did not.

## What remained domain-specific

- Miller needs resource identity, operator, access and service-level attribution decisions.
- Miller North needs allegation/finding/recommendation distinctions, Indigenous governance, anonymity safeguards and careful treaty/legal context.
- First Nations governance cannot be projected as a provincial or federal program merely because public funding exists.
- In Plain Sight implementation reporting requires recommendation-level status semantics that a resource backstory does not.

## Where traceability stopped

- New Roads reaches service-level funding and operation, but not audited expenditure or a final participation-results comparison.
- Creekside reaches a named operational expansion, while the public amount remains a provincewide program envelope.
- FNHO reaches recipient-level funding and operation, but not activity-level allocation of the later agreement.
- In Plain Sight reaches program-level funding and specific implementation examples, but not a current comprehensive status ledger for all 24 recommendations.

## Recurring evidence gaps

Across nine structured gaps:

- four require official-source follow-up;
- three are suitable for a narrow information-request candidate;
- one is a Miller owner/editorial decision;
- one is an ordinary bounded web-research question.

No gap was framed as proof of failure, noncompliance or ineffectiveness.

## Source-registry learning

The four dossiers used 11 source families across 19 dossier source references. High-yield families were B.C. government news for dated commitments, health-authority pages for current operations, FNHO pages for Indigenous governance and institutional reporting, B.C. Declaration Act reporting for current implementation limits, and federal proactive disclosure for recipient-level funding.

Government announcements were high-yield for amount, recipient and intent but low-yield for later operation. Current service pages were high-yield for operation but often lacked history or dated financial detail. Annual reports were useful for aggregate follow-up but rarely resolved service-level attribution.

One warranted source-family addition was made: Our Place Society program and service pages (`fogs_our_place_society`). It supports named-operator evidence for the New Roads canonical fixture. The private registry now has 30 families.

## Recommended reusable workflow

Keep the dossier as a shared adapter over domain-owned records. The next regression step should add a deliberately incomplete fixture and confirm that source-free edges, cross-domain ID substitution and unsafe scope elevation fail deterministically.
