# Legal / Finance Cross-Branch Integration

## Shared sequence

Incident or problem → recommendation → policy/legal response → commitment → funding → recipient → service/program → implementation → later financial, audit or outcome evidence.

## Reusable steps

- source identity and authority classification;
- evidence-bearing temporal relationships;
- recommendation and commitment IDs;
- organization identity;
- implementation depth;
- owner/publication gates;
- confidence and unresolved-question handling;
- significance prioritization and standard deliverables.

## Domain-specific projections

Miller adds resource identity, access, location, eligibility, capacity and current operation. Miller North adds incident/cohort, anonymity, Indigenous governance, treaty-context and accountability-stage semantics. Finance records must retain their originating domain even when the same federal or provincial program appears in both.

## Cross-branch boundary rules

1. A policy announcement can establish intended funding, not payment or service operation.
2. A grant or contract can establish recipient/value/purpose, not service effectiveness.
3. An operator page can establish current service information, not its share of a government envelope.
4. An audited entity statement can establish aggregate financial facts, not a selected service allocation.
5. Funding a First Nations organization does not confer governance authority on the funder.
6. Later audit findings must be attached to the exact program/recipient scope they assess.

## Recommendation

Keep the Legal & Government and Finance/Public Money branches as complementary read-only adapters over domain data. Share source/edge/gate contracts now; defer writable schema consolidation until amendment histories and service attribution are tested on a larger sample.
