# Miller northern and remote access foundation — 2026-09-08

## Purpose

This pass models the practical journey from a remote or northern community to care and back. It does not imply that every community has a local facility and does not encode clinical recommendations.

The reusable path is:

`origin → local access point → regional intake → destination → travel/funding → return-home support`

Each leg is included only where an official or first-party source supports it. Eligibility, approval and availability must still be confirmed by the administering service.

## Source review and durable outcomes

The bounded review produced 17 new canonical practical resources, enriched eight existing provincial/regional records, avoided six duplicate representations, and deferred five candidates that lacked enough source detail. A separate three-record high-ranking legacy refresh was completed; one imprecise directory record remained deferred.

Official systems reviewed:

- Manitoba Northern Health Region substance-use offices and The Pas RAAM/Rosaire House; Shared Health RAAM and MHA Connect; Manitoba Health Northern Patient Transportation Program.
- Ontario Ministry of Health Northern Health Travel Grant; Sioux Lookout First Nations Health Authority Nodin Mental Health, Client Services, telemedicine and NAN HOPE pathways.
- Newfoundland and Labrador Health Services patient navigation, Labrador-Grenfell ODT Hub and Humberwood Centre; provincial Medical Transportation Assistance Program; Nunatsiavut NIHB.
- Government of Nunavut community mental-wellness guidance and the 2025 Nunavut Medical Travel Handbook, including separate Qikiqtaaluk, Kivalliq and Kitikmeot contacts.
- Nova Scotia Health Recovery Support Centres and Health PEI community addiction services for rural/regional access enrichment.

Canonical source URLs are stored with each record in `miller-northern-remote-pathways-2026-09-08.json`.

## Regional findings

### Northern Manitoba

- The Pas has a verified walk-in RAAM access point at Rosaire House. Rosaire House remains a distinct substance-recovery centre and is not described as a withdrawal-management bed without direct confirmation.
- Flin Flon has a Northern Health Region Substance Use and Recovery office for local access and ongoing community connection.
- Churchill and other northern communities are represented through local providers, the provincial MHA Connect intake pathway and the Northern Patient Transportation Program when current eligibility and physician approval apply.
- Norway House, Cross Lake, Gillam, Swan River and other listed northern communities have place recognition and province/regional navigation, but most still need deeper community-specific service verification.

### Northwestern and northern Ontario

- Nodin provides self-referral, counselling, travelling counsellor and tele-counselling pathways for the First Nations served by SLFNHA.
- SLFNHA Client Services connects approved medical travel with hostel, local transportation, advocacy and discharge coordination in Sioux Lookout.
- NAN HOPE provides a 24/7 culturally relevant telephone navigation pathway for NAN communities.
- The Northern Health Travel Grant is represented as partial assistance with explicit referral, distance, nearest-service and other-coverage conditions—not as guaranteed reimbursement.

### Labrador and western Newfoundland

- The Labrador-Grenfell ODT Hub in Happy Valley-Goose Bay offers direct telephone, telehealth and in-person access without referral according to the current official service description.
- NL Health Services' provincial MHA Systems Navigator can connect addiction/mental-health needs with housing, income, community and justice supports.
- Nunatsiavut NIHB is a separate Indigenous benefit/medical-travel pathway with its own approval rules.
- Humberwood is represented as a physical Corner Brook destination, while MTAP is the provincial travel-assistance pathway. They are not presented as the same service.

### Nunavut

- Community health centres remain the home-community starting point.
- Iqaluit/Qikiqtaaluk, Rankin Inlet/Kivalliq and Cambridge Bay/Kitikmeot have distinct hub records and distinct regional medical-travel records. No hub or travel-office number is presented as the local number for every community or as the contact for another region.
- Medical travel follows the documented practitioner referral → community travel team → regional travel office → destination → return-home provider sequence.
- Current data supports navigation and travel coordination; it does not establish a local withdrawal or residential treatment facility in each community.

### Rural Atlantic Canada

- Nova Scotia's one provincial Recovery Support Centre program is enriched with its actual listed regional locations rather than cloned into each community.
- PEI's province-wide access line is connected to current service points in Charlottetown, Souris, Montague, Summerside and Alberton.
- Rural withdrawal, housing and treatment-transport pathways in New Brunswick, Cape Breton and western Newfoundland remain uneven and should be expanded only from stronger official source material.

## Return-home continuity

The mobile contract now supports a deterministic `return_home_after_treatment` workflow. It can surface verified housing, counselling, addiction-continuity, transportation and benefits-navigation options. Share packs can include concise regional intake, travel and return-home notes. The workflow does not create a clinical discharge plan or imply placement, coverage or acceptance.

## Coverage maturity

- Pilot-quality: British Columbia's existing deep foundation and selected Western regional pathways.
- Developing: Alberta and Saskatchewan; selected northern Manitoba, northwestern Ontario and Labrador pathways are useful but not province-complete.
- Foundation: Manitoba, Ontario, Newfoundland and Labrador, and rural Atlantic systems outside the researched seams.
- Exploratory: Nunavut remains exploratory despite materially improved region-specific access and travel pathways; local/community program depth is still limited.

## Verification and cadence

Use source-level refresh where one official page owns many locations or contacts:

- Northern Health Region office/RAAM listings: quarterly or when the index changes.
- SLFNHA program pages and Ontario travel-grant rules: quarterly URL/change checks plus milestone review when program rules change.
- NL Health Services navigation/facility pages and MTAP: quarterly to semi-annual, with change detection on access rules.
- Nunavut Medical Travel Handbook: annual/version change listener plus quarterly URL health; avoid individual daily community checks.
- Stable rural provincial intake pages: semi-annual unless source yield shows volatility.

## 211 complement

The remote-pathway gaps reinforce the value of a legitimate 211 partnership for basic community-service breadth, especially housing and local practical supports. It would not replace the official health-system, Indigenous medical-travel or treatment-access sources used here. No 211 data was scraped or ingested in this pass; the existing partnership/data-request approach remains the correct next step if permission, attribution and update terms become clear.
